# Behavioral: STAR stories

Graded against Checkout's principles, not technical depth (the interviewer may be a backend engineering manager, not a frontend specialist).
Hiring manager's warning: **most people forget the Result.** Give numbers and outcomes, and your personal impact.

## Template (~2 min per story)

- **S**ituation: 1–2 sentences. Context, stakes, who the customer was.
- **T**ask: your responsibility (and if it was *beyond* your scope, say so).
- **A**ction: what **you** did ("I", not "we"). 3–4 concrete steps, including the trade-off and why.
- **R**esult: numbers (time saved, errors ↓, conversion ↑, incidents ↓, users affected), plus what happened next.
- **L**esson: what you'd do differently, and what you now do by default.

A good story can cover 2 principles (for example, an incident story can show both customer focus and ownership). Have **at least 4 distinct stories** so you don't repeat one.

## Questions to ask them (pick 2–3)

- What does the Checkout frontend architecture look like today, and what's the biggest technical challenge for the next year?
- How do teams handle incidents? What's the on-call model for frontend engineers?
- What separates a senior engineer who's doing well here from one who's doing great?
- How are technical decisions made across teams (written design proposals, cross-team engineering groups)?
- What would you want me to have achieved in my first 6 months?
