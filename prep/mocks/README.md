# Mock interviews

Four timed simulations. They are the closest thing here to the real thing, and
they are worth more than any single drill — but only if you run them honestly.

## How to self-administer

1. **Open a blank TypeScript file** in your editor. Not a drill file. Not this
   repo's `prep/` folder. A scratch project that compiles, and nothing else.
2. **Set a real timer** to the stated length. Put your phone out of reach.
3. **Read only Part 1.** Do not scroll ahead. The follow-ups are timed for a
   reason — seeing them early removes the thing being tested, which is whether
   your first design left room for them.
4. **Talk out loud the whole time.** Record yourself if you can bear it; play
   back at 2× and count your silences. Ten seconds of silence is fine. Ninety
   is not.
5. **When the timer ends, stop.** Even mid-line. Then score yourself against
   `rubric.md` before reading the debrief.

## Which to run

| | Focus | Length | Run it on |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A** — Typed i18n helper | Type system, inference, API design | 45 min | Day 2 |
| **B** — Streaming client | Async, cancellation, iterators | 50 min | Day 4 |
| **C** — The race condition | React hooks, effects, correctness | 45 min | Day 5 |
| **D** — Language picker | Component API, keyboard, a11y | 50 min | Day 6 |

Short on time? **A and C** cover the most likely ground.

## Rules that make it worth doing

- No copy-pasting from `prep/`. If you need `deferred()`, write it.
- Looking something up is allowed — say out loud what you are looking up and
  why. That is what a real interviewer sees you do, and it is not a negative.
- If you finish early, do not stop: ask yourself the next follow-up and keep
  going. Finishing early with 20 minutes spare is itself information — the real
  round would have escalated.
- Score yourself before reading the debrief, or you will score the debrief.
